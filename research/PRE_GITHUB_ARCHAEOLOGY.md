# Pre-GitHub Archaeology Expedition

**Status:** live turn-for-turn observational record  
**Started:** 2026-09-29  
**Local home:** Crucible research  
**Working question:** Can we unzip a world that was never packed?

> **Turn-capture rule**
>
> While this expedition is active, every conversational turn that materially belongs to it should update this record before the collaboration advances. Preserve the turn close to where it occurs rather than relying on rolling chat context to carry it. Summaries and interpretations may accompany the turn, but should not silently replace evidence.
>
> This rule is expedition-local process, not a universal repository convention.

## Why this record exists

The collaboration has begun excavating pre-GitHub raster/conversation expeditions using machinery earned later: repository authority, provenance, semantic surfaces, selective recovery, executable worlds, and bounded local expedition records.

The historical experiments were not wordless. Their words remained available through rolling conversation context, while generated rasters carried additional world residue. What they lacked was an explicit durable, addressable preservation mechanism outside the conversation.

Git now changes the experiment: active context can become small without requiring the rest of the evidence to be either kept hot or discarded.

The expedition should not reconstruct the entire old chronology in advance. Begin from surviving shards, recover deeper evidence only when present pressure requires it, act where useful, and preserve what this new encounter earns.

Candidate loop:

**residue → need → excavation → reconstruction → new experience → new residue**

Old rasters are evidence, not specifications.

### Model interpretation

The earlier description of a reset/discard boundary was too strong.

The **visible working surface** could collapse while the broader historical material remained latent in rolling conversation context. The rasters also preserved consequences in the world itself.

This exposes at least three historical continuity channels:

- **working surface** — the small current set of handles/tokens;
- **latent conversational context** — prior words still recoverable inside the active rolling context;
- **world residue** — consequences that persisted in later rasters.

What was missing was a fourth channel now supplied by Git:

- **durable provenance** — cold, addressable evidence that can survive conversation discontinuity and be selectively recovered later.

A useful present interpretation is therefore:

**hot:** what this encounter needs  
**warm:** locally reformed working context  
**cold:** provenance-bearing evidence  
**world:** consequences that actually survived

None of these needs to be promoted prematurely into a universal memory architecture.

The strongest correction is:

> A tiny active surface does not imply that only its contents survived. It may mean only that those contents needed to be carried *now*.

This reframes the old experiments as probes of bounded attention more than bounded existence.

Livesey's notebooks are especially notable in retrospect: a character with incomplete local knowledge observes, records uncertainty, separates questions from evidence, revises over time, and constructs a locally useful account of a world whose complete history he does not possess. This resembles later ledgers, semantic surfaces, bounded apertures, and selective recovery, but resemblance is not proof that the old work implemented the later architecture.

Candidate archaeological finding:

**The recent work may not have invented the continuity idea so much as supplied the durable persistence substrate the earlier experiments lacked.**

This remains an interpretation to test against further recovered evidence.

### Consequence

The expedition now adopts the turn-capture rule in its header.

This is deliberately stronger than the old rolling-context mechanism: the conversation remains useful working context, but Git becomes the durable expedition ledger. Each relevant turn should leave repository residue while the expedition is live.

The purpose is not exhaustive transcript preservation for its own sake. The purpose is to prevent interpretation from outrunning evidence and to make later selective recovery possible from the actual local expedition record.
